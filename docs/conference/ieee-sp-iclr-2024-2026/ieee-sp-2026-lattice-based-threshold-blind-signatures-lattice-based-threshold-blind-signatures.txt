#### Abstract

Blind signatures are a central tool for privacypreserving protocols. They allow users to obtain signatures from a signer without the signer seeing the signed message. For instance, it enables electronic cash: signatures correspond to coins which can be issued by the bank in a privacypreserving manner via blind signing. To mitigate the risk of key compromise, threshold blind signatures allow the distribution of the signing key amongst N$N$ parties. While recent works have focused on improving the security of this primitive in the classical setting, no construction is known to date in the post-quantum setting. We present the first construction of a threshold blind signature secure in the post-quantum setting, based on lattices. We prove its security under an interactive variant of the SIS assumption introduced in [Agrawal et al., CCS'22]. Our construction has a reasonable overhead of a factor of roughly 1.4×$1.4 \times$ to 2.5×$2.5 \times$ in signature size over comparable nonthreshold blind signatures over lattices under heuristic but natural assumptions.
